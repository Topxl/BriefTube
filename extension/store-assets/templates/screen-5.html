Morning sunlight within 30 to 60 minutes of waking sets the circadian clock via specialized retinal cells, anchoring cortisol and melatonin release to a consistent rhythm.

Even overcast mornings provide enough photons. A daily 5 to 10 minute walk outdoors outperforms any artificial light protocol tested in the lab.

Evening bright overhead lights delay sleep onset. Dimming below eye level after sunset preserves hormonal rhythm without affecting productivity.
